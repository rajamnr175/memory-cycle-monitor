The Amazon note trade

Loans that turn into shares at old prices

Amazon is owed about $6.2bn on convertible notes that can become up to 1.84bn shares (an average of ≈$3.37 a share). Its own filings valued them at $97.9bn on 30 June. It kept its 12% ownership cap for the IPO, so only $579.3m converts now, into ≈276.8m non-voting Class N shares (≈$2.09 a share); the rest converts each quarter after listing, still under the cap, which Amazon can waive. Amazon has put about $18bn into Anthropic in total, and Anthropic has committed at least $110bn to AWS over 10 years.

What happened

Why the stocks fell while earnings rose

No company has cut guidance. The selling is about what investors expect profits to look like in 2027–2028, plus a set of market shocks stacked on top of each other.

    The six reasons, ranked

    • Peak-earnings fear. Memory is the most cyclical part of semis. Margins of 75–87% are far above any prior cycle, and investors sell before the turn, not after it. Goldman's desk says peak-earnings worry is capping valuations. This is why the stocks trade at 4–8× run-rate earnings.
    • Supply is coming. SK hynix (₩100tn central-Korea plan, Yongin cleanroom early 2027, M15X pulled in), Samsung (₩140tn group plan), Micron (new cleanrooms in 2028) and Toshiba in HDDs are all adding capacity. TrendForce sees DRAM/HBM supply up 27% in 2H27.
    • China. CXMT reached ~8% of global DRAM revenue in Q1 2026 (Counterpoint), listed in July, and on 12–13 Oct said it has a 4F² cell design for DDR5 server modules by end-2026. That design packs ~50% more bits per wafer. It does not touch HBM, and US tool export limits still apply.
    • AI capex durability. Nvidia's reported ~$250bn backstop for an OpenAI project (circular financing), Meta reselling compute, and Anthropic's CEO calling for slower capability growth (14 Sep) all hit the AI-demand story that memory sits on.
    • Rates. The Bank of Korea hiked in July. US September CPI ran hot and odds of an October Fed hike jumped from 22% to 46% (LSEG). Higher rates compress cyclical multiples.
    • Flows. Samsung and SK hynix were about half of the KOSPI. Their ₩55tn of buybacks ran ahead of schedule and ended in early–mid October, foreign funds sold for five straight sessions, and the KOSPI hit sidecars and halts repeatedly.
    Outlook

    What the order books say

    Bull case: demand is contracted

    • Micron: ~75% of 2027 output already committed; most sales talks now about 2028. 26 multi-year customer agreements expected to cover >35% of revenue through 2030. CEO: 2027 and 2028 tighter than 2026, "no line of sight" to balance. $12.75bn in customer deposits.
    • HBM pricing: TrendForce sees 2027 HBM contract prices up 70–140%; Samsung is seeking more than double current HBM4 prices from Nvidia and Broadcom.
    • Near-term prices still rising: 4Q26 conventional DRAM contract +10–15% QoQ, NAND +15–20% (TrendForce, 30 Sep).
    • LTAs: SK hynix now has long-term agreements with 14 customers covering more than half of its 2027–28 capacity; Samsung has 10 large LTAs with 10 more in talks; Kioxia targets 50% LTA coverage of 2028 volume; SanDisk signed 5 more contracts since April; Seagate nearline capacity allocated through 2027.
    • Balance sheets: Micron $73.5bn cash vs $5.2bn debt; SK hynix ₩69.4tn net cash; Samsung ₩167.6tn net cash; SanDisk zero debt.

    Bear case: price, not volume, is the risk

    • Revenue growth is mostly price. At SanDisk two-thirds of the last sequential jump came from price, one-third from volume. When prices stop rising, growth stops fast.
    • Price ceilings: some LTAs signed after Q2 2026 cap further increases, so contracts protect volume more than margin.
    • Demand destruction: cloud firms are testing smaller HBM loads in custom AI chips; Nvidia is reportedly testing reduced HBM specs for Rubin Ultra. Consumer is already weak: SanDisk consumer revenue fell 32% QoQ, Samsung's phone unit lost money on component costs.
    • Execution misses: SK hynix Q2 revenue and operating profit missed consensus by ~5%; Samsung Q3 revenue missed by ~3%. At these levels "record" is not enough.
    • Sensitivity: halving expected earnings doubles the P/E with no price change. A 5× P/E on peak earnings is not cheap if earnings fall 50%.

    Reading it: at today's prices the memory makers are priced as if profits fall by roughly half or more within two years, which is what happened in every past cycle. The HDD makers (Seagate, Western Digital) trade at roughly 20–23× because their supply is tight and their pricing is more stable. If memory profits only drift down 20–30%, the stocks look cheap; if the 2023-style collapse repeats, they do not.
